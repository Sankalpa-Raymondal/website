# Bancharamer Bagan (1980)
*Directed by Tapan Sinha · ★★★★★ · Watched October 2026*
## Oh my, how Bengali!
I love this movie! A great ending.
<!--more-->
On 2026 Summer, I talked with my Shona Dadu in Mamabari. He recommended this movie to me, and his daughter then commented that she too liked this movie. She kept saying "loose character", which I found quite memorable.
I watched this movie in parts on youtube: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wW-CvjeNWbc . It is quite an entertaining movie. Let me just word vomit all the things I feel about it. Do not expect good writing. 

Okay, setup. Movie starts with a normal dude and his grandson tending a garden. Kinda wholesome. The land is a big thing for Bengal. And to be honest, I believe Bengal is so fertile that it is possible to have such a beautiful garden there. Through talent, hardwork and pure love for gardening, Banchara makes a great garden. I believe the best things in the world are so because they have love in it. The creator thinks about how much joy this thing will give to people and puts in extra attention to creating things. People who only care about money just put in enough effort to not get out of their job, or their business. I believe Bancharam lives with love, with his dear grandson (at that time) and with his garden.

And then we move to the Zamindar house, with the dude with literally bulging out eyes. Now these guys have lots of money, compared to the peasants mentioned previously. Haha, it is literally a baron and a peasant sort of thing going on here. This guy with the eyes popping out is refered to as Boro Korta. I do not really know what Korta is, but in my mother's side of the family the patriarch was called Korta, and Boro means big. Boro Korta is an asshole and he lives a pathetic existence, even though he is such a big Zamindar and everything. He owns all the land around him, and treats everyone around him poorly. The first impression of him from the movie is him getting pissed off at the "Bou" brought from the Boubazar and the entourage of singers for some reason. 
Boro Korta should be called Big Baby actually. He keeps throwing temper tantrums and never really admits to mistakes. He calls a tree a mango tree, and the sycophant "Manager" around him corrects him and says it is actually a litchie tree. The Big Baby says, "If I say it is a mango tree, it is a mango tree." What an annoying person. Another big thing for Boro Korta, is that he cannot sleep. Probably because he is a terrible person who make life miserable to the people around him. He stays up all night and makes his Manager stay up all night too to keep him entertained, or to help him sleep. He abruptly orders this servants for a carriage to be taken out for his "Morning Walk", because his doctor told him to go for early morning walks.

During this "walk", he sees a peasant couple and arranges for the woman to meet him later for his pleasure. He treats people like his property. He then comes across the beautiful garden and he is surprised that such a garden exists in his property. I mean he must be, because all his life he would only get things that money can pay for, and money cannot pay for love. Apparently Bancharam's ancestor saved Big Korta's ancestors from a tiger and this land was gifted to Bancharam's family. The Big Baby wants this land and assigns this task to his Manager. The Manager subcontracts this task to a muscular Gunda. This Gunda is quite iconic because he speaks Hindi mixed with Bengali. He underestimates Bancharam and tries to intimidate him. Bancharam takes him by surprise and wards him away. They come back with a big swarm of goons and Bancharam has to run away. 

Now the unrealistic part is that a British officer sees this and intervenes. He helps Bancharam by giving him protection and warns Boro Korta never to touch Bancharam's Bagan (garden) again. Maybe the British belief of private property supercedes everything else. If the people believe that property can be taken away by force, then what prevents people to join together and fight for it? But I still find it hard to believe that the British, who came to take over the whole of India, have a problem if a zamindar wants to take over some peasant's garden. But I guess hypocrisy has always been in fashion.

Okay, I cannot go for the full story. Just watch the movie.

Money money money. Boro Korta and his son, Korta, all believe in money and getting more of it. They live miserable lives full of toxic people around them. Even Bancharam's grandson loves money especially because he can spend it for his pleasure, and wants to remove Bancharam's life work as the garden and replace it with an alcohol distillery. How ironic that a garden with cauliflowers, mangos, litchies, brinjals and watermelons will be replaced with posion that makes you feel good for a while. Like this chase of money makes lives miserable. Look at Korta's sons. They are pieces of shit. Korta himself tells his wife, "How did the union of you and me create these goats?". Korta's wife is also a nutcase in some regards. At one point of the movie, Korta and his wife are happy because Bancharam allegedly died, with Korta jumping around in joy. Like a child who got something he/she wished for. And it was hilariously awkward when Bancharam hobbles into the house. It is Korta's goal in life to get more property, even though he already has so much property already. And in the chase of more money and power and property, he has lost his humanity. He is literally a demon. 
Even the grandson's wife only married him only after lied about how much money is on his way. You should have seen how her eyes started glowing and how she started acting all coy and stuff. 

The dialogues in this movie are great. The Korta calls everyone a "loose character", being the biggest loose character himself. He will do bad things to you, but make sure it is legal. I do not know how he justifies wanting the murder of someone as legal, but sure what the hell. Towards the end he goes desperate and even gives Bancharam poison for him to drink.

Bancharam acts stupid and weak to survive as an old peasant in the zamindar world. He cannot use physical force or luck anymore. He needs to use his wits. He takes care of the land, and the land takes care of him. There is a scene where a character tells him that Bancharam is not as simple as he looks. Ofcourse the character who says this also a despicable person who mocks an old man who might almost die.

Everyone mocks Bancharam, but Bancharam always has the last say. This old dude with dark skin and white hair and beard is really witty and just knows what to say to rile the Korta up. Like how the Zamindars sucked the peasants dry, this Bancharam literally made a Zamindar straight up deliver cash to him. Bancharam is like Don, he is always two steps ahead of everyone.

One thing that bothers me is how poorly we treat women as Bengalis. All a drunk father can hope for is to marry his daughter off. Can't the daughter do something of her own? There was no space for women in this movie except as a "Bou". What a shit society.

But this grandson's wife is a pretty good addition to Bancharam's family. She does stand up for what is right and actually does some work, even though she is selfish. And I mean, she gives birth to Bancharam's great grandson. She even expands the garden I believe, and saves Bancharam from death by helping to remove blockage from his wind pipe or something. Thank God there were subtitles for this movie. 

I think even more than this being the win for peasants, this movive is about the destructive nature of believing in money. The Zamindars only drink and they do not have any love in the way they treat themselves or the people around them. Even for guys that Bancharam knows, they only want him alive because this gives them access to his garden.

I got to learn some new Bengali words and ways of speaking from this movie. And it is nice to see a movie with depictions of some vices too, because they are indeed part of reality. Most of the media I was shown when growing up was some clean and safe media. 

I got to go now. I am kind of sleepy too. I am done for now.
